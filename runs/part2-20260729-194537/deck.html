Worked Example 23.1: Temporary Elevator Sizing

🏗 Application

A temporary elevator assembled at a construction site to raise building materials.

⚙ Given Parameters
  • Load to be raised (W): 5 kN
  • Lifting height (h): 20 m
  • Acceleration (a): 1 m/s²
  • Rope Construction: 6×19 with fibre core
  • Nominal Diameter (dr): 10 mm
  • Tensile Designation: 1570
  • Preliminary Factor of Safety (fs): 10
🎯 Objective

Determine the number of wire ropes (z) required, neglecting bending stresses.

Step I: Weight of the material
Distributed evenly across z ropes:
Material weight per rope = (5000/z) N
Step II: Weight of the wire rope
From Table 23.4, mass of a 100m long 10mm rope (6×19 fibre core) is 34.6 kg.
Weight of the wire = (34.6 × 20 / 100) × 9.81
Result = 67.89 N

Example 23.1: Acceleration Force and Resolution

Step III: Force due to acceleration
Force = (Mass of material + Mass of rope) × Acceleration
Mass of material per rope = [(5000/9.81)(1/z)]
Mass of each rope = [34.6 × 20 / 100]
Force due to acceleration = [(5000/9.81)(1/z) + (34.6 × 20 / 100)] × 1
Result = (509.68/z) + 6.92 N
Step IV: Number of wire ropes
From Table 23.4, the breaking strength of the rope is 48 kN. With fs = 10:

48000 / 10 = 5000/z + 67.89 + 509.68/z + 6.92
4800 = 5509.68/z + 74.81
z = 1.166
Final Answer: 2 wire ropes

Worked Example 23.2: Accounting for Bending Stress

⚙ Objective

Assume the data of Example 23.1 (5 kN load, 10 mm 6×19 rope, z = 2) and determine the true factor of safety by taking bending stresses into account.

🏗 Given

Sheave diameter D = 45 dr

Step I: Bending load (Pb)
From Eq. 23.3 and Table 23.7 for a 6×19 rope:
• Er = 83000 N/mm²
• dw = 0.063 dr
• A = 0.40 dr²

Pb = AErdw/D = [(0.40 dr²)(83000)(0.063 dr)] / (45 dr)
Pb = 46.48 dr² = 46.48 (10)²
Result = 4648 N

Example 23.2: True Factor of Safety

Step II: Total load on wire rope
The total force consists of the factors from Example 23.1 plus the bending load (Pb).
Because z=2, the external loads are halved per rope:
Ptotal = (5000/2) + 67.89 + (509.68/2) + 6.92 + 4648
Result = 7477.65 N
Step III: Factor of safety
Using the established breaking strength of 48 kN (48,000 N): fs = 48000 / 7477.65
Result = 6.42
Engineering Takeaway: Neglecting bending stress suggested a safety factor of 10. Including the severe realities of bending reduces the true safety factor to 6.42.

Worked Example 23.3: Fatigue Life Calculation

Application: Raising a 20 kN load over a two-part line sheave.

Given Parameters:
  • Load (W): 20 kN
  • Usage Rate: 500 bends per week
  • Rope: 12 mm (dr), 6×19 fibre core
  • Tensile Designation: 1570
  • Sheave Pitch Diameter (D): 500 mm
Step I: The (p/Sut) factor
Each side of the two-part line shares a load of (20/2) = 10 kN.

p = 2P / drD = 2(10×10³) / (12×500) = 3.33 N/mm²

Sut = 1570 N/mm² (ultimate tensile strength)
Result: p/Sut = 3.33 / 1570 = 0.0021

Example 23.3: Resolving Expected Life

Step II: Life of wire rope
Using the calculated p/Sut ratio of 0.0021, we reference the experimental fatigue data for a 6×19 regular-lay rope (Figure 23.6).
• Bends to failure: 3.30 × 105 bends.

Given the operational frequency of 500 bends per week:
Life in weeks = (3.30 × 105) / 500
Life in years = (3.30 × 105) / (500 × 52)
Final Answer: 12.69 years

Worked Example 23.4: Hoist Design for Long Fatigue Life

Application: Select a rope and sheave diameter based on fatigue failure criteria.

Given Parameters:
  • Hoist + Material Weight (W): 5 kN (5000 N)
  • Depth (h): 100 m
  • Velocity (v2): 5 m/s (attained from v1=0 in t=5 s)
  • Rope Target: 6×19, Tensile Designation 1570
Step I: Total initial load (P) estimation
We must assume a trial rope mass to begin iterative sizing.
Trial Mass = 40 kg / 100 m
• Weight of wire rope = 40 × 9.81 = 392.4 N
• Acceleration = (v2 - v1)/t = (5-0)/5 = 1 m/s²
• Acceleration force = [(5000/9.81) + 40] × 1 = 549.68 N
Total P = 5000 + 392.4 + 549.68 = 5942.08 N

Example 23.4: Sizing via Fatigue Criterion

Step II: Value of (p) for long fatigue life
For long fatigue life, the ratio p/Sut must be ≤ 0.0015. Sut = 1570 N/mm².
p = 0.0015 × Sut = 0.0015 × 1570. Target p = 2.355 N/mm²
Step III: Nominal diameter of wire rope (dr)
From Table 23.7, recommended sheave diameter D = 45dr
p = 2P/drD ⇒ 2.355 = 2P/dr(45dr), dr² = 2P/(2.355×45) = P/52.99, dr = √(5942.08/52.99) = 10.59 mm
Selection: Round up to standard nominal diameter dr = 12 mm.
Sheave Diameter (D) = 45 × 12 = 540 mm

Example 23.4: Design Verification

Step IV: Check iterative trial
Recalculating with the actual 12 mm rope mass of 49.8 kg / 100 m (from Table 23.4).
• True weight of wire: 49.8 × 9.81 = 488.54 N
• True acceleration force: [(5000/9.81) + 49.8] × 1 = 559.48 N
True Total P: 5000 + 488.54 + 559.48 = 6048.02 N
Re-evaluating Fatigue Criteria:
p = 2P/drD = 2(6048.02)/(12×540) = 1.867 N/mm²
p/Sut = 1.867/1570 = 0.00119 (< 0.0015, Validates long life)
Step V: Static design factor of safety
From Table 23.4, the minimum breaking load of a 12 mm, 6×19 fibre core rope is 69 kN (69,000 N).

fs = Breaking Load / P = 69000 / 6048.02
Result: fs = 11.4

Hardware Design: Rope Sheaves and Drums

The physical hardware guiding and winding the rope is just as critical as the rope itself. There are three primary design requirements:

↖↗
↙↘

1. Proper Size: Sheaves and drums must be as large as possible to maximize rope life.

↻

2. Free Rotation: They must run freely on their axles to minimize friction.

◯

3. Accurate Grooves: They must possess correctly dimensioned grooves to support the rope without pinching.

The Design Trade-Off
While a larger diameter exponentially increases rope life by reducing bending stresses, it directly increases manufacturing cost and generates significantly higher centrifugal forces at high operational speeds.

Sheave Material Selection & Construction

Rope life depends heavily on the contacting material of the sheave groove.

The Wear Ratio Matrix
Grey Cast Iron (FG 200)
  • Preferred Material
  • Baseline wear on rope: 1.0
  • Naturally dampens friction
Mild / Welded Steel
  • Secondary alternative
  • Increased wear on rope: ~1.1
  • Steel sheaves cause ropes to wear faster
Construction Specs:
  • Small Sheaves: Cast as one-piece construction without supporting ribs.
  • Large Sheaves: Cast with structural ribs.
  • Mounting: Always freely mounted on fixed axles using either rolling-contact bearings or bronze bushings.

Sheave Groove Geometry & Failure Modes

Standard Dimensions
  • Groove Radius (R): 1.05 × nominal rope radius
  • Groove Depth: 2 × dr (nominal rope diameter)
  • Bottom Arc: Circular arc sweeping 120°
  • Side Flare Angle: Included angle between 40° to 45°
Oversize Groove (Too Large R)
  • Loss of lateral support.
  • Wire rope flattens and deforms under heavy load.
Undersize Groove (Too Small R)
  • Causes rope distortion and extreme pinching.
  • Strands/wires bind against each other, multiplying internal friction.
  • Forces individual wires to carry unequal, excessive shares of the load.

Rope Drums: Construction and Sizing

Grooved vs. Plain Drums
  • Machined helical grooves are strictly preferred over plain cylindrical drums.
  • Why? Grooves increase the bearing surface, eliminate inter-turn friction, prevent jamming, and drastically reduce wear.
  • Material: Grey Cast Iron Grade FG 200.
  • Configuration: Single right-hand helical groove for one rope; right+left-hand helical grooves for two ropes.
Groove Pitch (t)
t = dr + (2 to 3 mm)
Groove Diameter
Dia. = dr + (1.5 to 3 mm)
Shell Thickness (t1)
t1 = 0.02D + (6 to 10 mm)

Synthesis: The End-to-End Design Procedure

(1) Application Setup
Define load, height, velocity. Select rope construction (6×7, 6×19) & material (Sut).
(2) Total Load Calculation
Compute load sum: Dead Weight + Rope Weight + Acceleration Forces.
(3) Static Safety
Verify Static fs against requirements in Tables 23.5/23.6.
(4) Bending Load
Determine sheave diameter D. Compute equivalent bending load Pb (Eq. 23.3).
(5) Fatigue Verification
Compute interface pressure p. Verify fatigue via p/Sut ≤ 0.0015 target (Fig. 23.6).
(6) Hardware Sizing
Detail sheave groove geometry (radius/depth/angle) and drum shell dimensions (pitch/thickness).
